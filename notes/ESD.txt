WHY ESD protection
	static electrical charge will accumulate during device operation
		This results in large ESD event
	
	Strategic placement of ESD protection helps defends against suche vents

Unidirectional TVS Diode
	Transient voltage suppression, protects circuit from strong transient voltage (i.e. electrostatic discharge)
		i.e. if ESD event is strong enough
			diode activates into reverse bias, thus affectively directing the current flow to GND instead of the subcircuit
	Will be some current leakage, realistically (refer to the tvs diode graph, similar to diode graph)
Think as the diode as a open circuit, when ESD event not occur
	Then it becomes short to GND when it occurs

Bidirectional TVS Diode
	Same as unidirectional, but works in both direction
	protects against both cases where the GND net has some high impedance or fault

Honestly think for power rails, i.e. the nets that we are using for the external connectors, unidirectionals hould suffice
	Place TVS diodes as close to the connectors as possible

Relays for ESD protection

When looking at specs
	PAY ATTENTION TO CLAMPING VOLTAGE
		Lower clamping voltage means better protection for components

Lower impedance from ESD source to GND as much as possible, follow guidelines
Keep Angles < 45 degrees

Grounding schemes are critical in protecting against ESD
Separate Chassis GND, digital, analog GND
Electrical connections to a Earth/GND chassis through PCB grounded through-holes adjacent to TVS ground nad ESD ground

Summarize:

• Controlling impedances around the TVS for dissipating ESD current, IESD
• Limiting the effects of EMI on unprotected circuits
• Properly using VIAs to maximize ESD dissipation by the TVS
• Designing a grounding scheme which has very low impedance for the TVS

PCB Layout guidlines for ESD dissipation
consider parasitic inductance
	Ideally want parasitic inductance to be greated from TVS to protected circuit, then from ESD source to TVS.
	This can be achieved by reducing the distance between the TVS diode and ESD source

No VIA should be placed on the trace that shorts the ESD current to GND
	Remember whant traces to be no more than 45 degrees
ESD source typically comes from connector in PCB


Limiting EMI from ESD
	radiation source will be the circuit b/w ESD source and TVS.
	Consider this keep-out region for unprotected PCB traces
	Summary
		dont route unprotected circuits in areas b/w ESD Source and TVS
		Place TVS as near to connector as possible
		Route w/ straight traces b/w ESD source and TVS

Routing with vias
	Avoid VIAS b/w ESD source and TVS if possible
	Case 1:
		No via b/w ESD source and TVS, i.e. ESD source and TVS Diode are on same plane
		Thus ESD must flow from TVS before protected IC
	Case 2:
		Via is in b/w Protected and ESD source
		This increases the impedance from the ESD source to TVS Diode
		So current will flow straight to the circuit

Grounding Schemes for ESD
	TVS GND pin should connect to GND plane that is stitched to an adjacent GND plane layer
	Lower the L3 parasitic inductance

Choosing the right TVS Diode
Key Parameters
	VRWM
		Applied voltage where TVS leakage is specified to be very low, in the nano-amps (very negligible)
			Voltage applied to TVS where diode doesn't conduct significant current during use (i.e. < 100 nA)
		SHOULD BE ABOVE MAXIMUM OPERATING VOLTAGE OF WHATEVER COMPONENT ITS PROTECTING
	VBR
		Applied voltage where TVS will see 1mA leakage and begin shunting current
		I.e. in active region
		Essentially is the inflection point of I/V curve, where current increases exponentially
		DC value and can shift depending on process and temperature
		VRWM < VBR, So set VRWM as the max threshold for current to operate not VBR
	IPP
		Maximum current for defined waveform that TVS diode can sustain before failure
		Ensure Ipp of diode is larger than peak surge current so no TVS failure occurs
		defined relative to waveform
	VCLAMP
		Voltage that TVS diode regulats to when exposed to transient current
		lower the Vclamp the less chance of failure due to electrical overstress
		V_clamp = V_BR + R_DYN * I_SURGE
		V_clamp is affected heavily by r_dyn
		Don't know if we need to worry to much abt this
	RDYN
		Resistance in TVS diode that causes Vclamp to rise when exposed to transient current
		Diode intrsic property
	Polarity
		Whether TVS sustains +/- V_RWM or only a positive V_RWM w/o current leakage
		Unidirectional IV Curve
			Used in applicaitons where voltage is always positive (power nets?)
			More robust protection from negative surge pulses
		Bidirectional IV Curve
			Voltage can be positive or negative.
			Provide smore flexibility for input voltages
	I_leak
		Leakage current when voltage is applied, specced at V_RWM
	Capacitance
		Parasitic capacitance in TVS diode that can impact system operation.
