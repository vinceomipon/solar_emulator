Choosing the correct TVS Diode
	**Key specs to look for in data sheet**

VRWM: applied voltage
	TVS leakage is specified to be very low in the nano amps
	In design, param should be above the expected max operating voltage.
		diode leak increases significantly if applied voltage exceeds
		this param (see I/V curve)
	Ex. protected line is at 5V nominal, max variance up to 7V,  
		ensure V_RWM is 7V or greater

VBR: Applied voltage 
	where TVS will have 1mA leakage and begin shunting current.
	inflection point on I/V diode curve, whgere leakage increase
	exponentially
	DC value, can shift depending on temp and process
	In design, make sure **nominal voltage** stays below V_RWM NOT V_BR
		This is to assure very low system leakage
	Select a diode where I_pp references your exact waveform used for test
	conditions
Ipp: Max current for a defined waveform before TVS diode go to failure
	i.e. the max current the diode can shunt before diode is damaged
	In design, I_pp should be much higher than circuit peak surge current

Vclamp: voltage that TVS diode regulates to when exposed to transient current.
	determines voltage system will be exposed to during a surce
		Lower v_clamp implies less failure of protectred circuit
		V_clamp is determined by R_DYN so choose diode with lower
		R_Dyn
	V_clamp specified in diode datasheet relative to I_surge and a
	reference waveform
		Match data sheet test conditions to your own
Rdyn: resistance in diode that causes Vclamp to rise when exposed to transient
current
	R_Dyn is diode intrinsic property (i.e property of diode itself)
	V_clamp = V_BR + I_surge x R_dyn
	If R_dyn not specified in datasheet can be derived by
	us eqn above to derive R_dyn

Polarity:
	Whether TVS can sustain a +/- V_RWM or only a positive V_RWM w/o
	leakage current
	Observe the differences in I/V curve b/w unidirectional and
	bidirectional configs
		Use unidirection for applications wher e voltage is always
		positive
			More robust protection against negative surge pulses
		Use bidirectional where voltage can be positive or negative
			More flexibility for input voltages
		negative V_clamp of unidirecitonal TVS diode much better than
		V_clamp of bidirectional TVS diode

I_leak:
	leakage current when voltage is applied, specced at VRWM
Capacitance:
	parasitic capacitance in TVS diode that impact system operation

**Refer to the TVS Diode I/V curve**

Voltage tolerances by rail (according to gemini)
• +3.3V Rail: ±5% (Range: +3.135V to +3.465V)
• +5V Rail: ±5% (Range: +4.750V to +5.250V)
• +12V Rail: ±5% to -7% depending on the standard (Range: roughly +11.16V to
+12.60V
